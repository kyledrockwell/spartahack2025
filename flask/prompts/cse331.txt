CSE 331 covers: 

In this course, you will gain a strong theoretical and conceptual understanding of common data structures and algorithms, as well as how to apply them within larger programming projects. 

Specific topics we will cover include: 

*   **Asymptotic analysis,** **Algorithm Analysis, Recursion, and recurrence relations**.  

*   **Sorting** and divide-and-conquer. 

*   **Data structures and ADTs:** lists, stacks, queues, sets, dictionaries, linked lists, arrays, trees, balanced trees, AVL trees, Red Black Trees, Maps and Hash tables, Priority queues, binary heaps, and disjoint sets.  

*   **Graphs and graph algorithms:** graph search, shortest path, and minimum spanning trees. A\* Search. 

*   **Greedy algorithms, Dynamic Programming.** 

This course is also designed to have a practical component to help you gain basic familiarity with techniques used within industry. You’ll be asked to: 

*   Work on **programming projects** and integrate your work in an existing codebase. 

*   Learn how to use an **IDE**. 

*   Learn techniques for **checking correctness**: using our unit tests with PyCharm debugging, designing, and checking invariants, etc. 

CSE 331 Course objectives: 

Students completing this course are expected to be able to: 

*   Understand several significant algorithms 

*   Compare and select among fundamental data structures  

*   Apply general algorithms to practical problems 

*   Analysis algorithm complexity 

*   Study of common complexity classes  

*   Greedy Algorithms: Dijkstra's algorithm, Prim’s, Knuth’s algorithm, Minimum spanning trees 

*   Divide and conquerDynamic programming

Make-Up Examination Policy 

In alignment with our commitment to support our students during unforeseen and challenging circumstances, we have established the following make-up examination policy. This policy is designed to accommodate students who are unable to attend scheduled examinations due to serious and unavoidable reasons. A make-up examination is a privilege, not a right, and is granted only under the following specific conditions: 

*   **Medical Emergencies**: Students who are unable to attend an examination due to hospitalization or serious medical conditions must provide a valid hospital report. The report should clearly indicate the student's incapacity to attend the examination on the scheduled date. 

*   **Legal Obligations**: Students who are unable to attend an examination due to mandatory legal obligations, such as court appearances, must provide a valid police report or a court document. The document should clearly state the date and nature of the legal obligation. 

*    **Bereavement**: Students who have experienced the loss of an immediate family member (parent, sibling, spouse, or child) may be granted a make-up examination. Approval for a make-up examination under these circumstances requires a grief absence authorization granted by the Engineering Department. It is the student's responsibility to communicate with the department and obtain the necessary authorization in a timely manner. 

*   **MSU** **Class** Conflicts: Students who have a verified scheduling conflict with another **MSU** class are eligible for a make-up examination. Documentation confirming the class conflict, such as an official class schedule or a note from the registrar, must be provided. 

*   **Additional Notes:** 

*   The make-up exam may differ in format and content from the original, including the possibility of an oral exam. 

*   The availability of a make-up exam is at the instructor's discretion based on your specific circumstances. 

*   By including this make-up examination policy in the syllabus, we aim to provide clarity and support to our students while maintaining the integrity and academic standards of our courses. We urge all students to read and understand this policy thoroughly and adhere to its provisions.

Grasping Course Content & Upholding Academic Integrity for Career Success 

Understanding your algorithms and data structures is important in obtaining a good job; your success in this class will be the foundation for your career in your future. 

Why Upholding Academic Integrity Matters: 

1\. **Deep Understanding Over Surface Learning**: The complexities of Algorithms and Data Structures are not to be merely memorized but understood. Employers are looking for individuals who can apply, adapt, and innovate, not just regurgitate facts. 

2\. **Authentic Skillset**: By ensuring that you genuinely grasp the content, you present an authentic version of yourself during job interviews. This honesty is evident, and recruiters can differentiate between those who truly know and those who don’t. 

3\. **Professional Reputation**: Upholding integrity means building a reputation for honesty and reliability. In the tech industry, your reputation precedes you. Missteps can follow you throughout your career, limiting opportunities and partnerships. 

The Imperative of Solo Mastery: 

While collaborative discussions are valuable and encouraged, every project in this course should be the result of your individual effort. It's not just about completing assignments; it's about genuinely internalizing knowledge to stand out in job interviews. Engaging in solo work ensures that your grasp is not only genuine but also robust enough to tackle real-world challenges. Remember, in many interviews, it’s this individual prowess in Algorithms and Data Structures that will set you apart.
